Inside the denoiser: networks you already know

So far the network was a box. Goal of this part: open it: a U-Net for pixels, a Transformer for patches, and why big models denoise a compressed image.

What kind of prediction is this?

Same shape in, same shape out: a dense prediction

→network+ t→ ⇒

Like segmentation, there is one output per pixel. Unlike segmentation, each output is a real number (noise), not a class.

So the architectures that work for dense prediction (encoder–decoders with skips) work here.

Input and output look alike, both noise-like. That is expected: the output is noise.

Subtracting it shows what the network "saw": our 7.

Recall the segmentation lecture: per-pixel class scores. Here it is per-pixel regression. Same need for local detail plus global context.

The network that made every sample in this lecture

A small U-Net: shrink to see context, grow back for detail

{{unet_params}} parameters. The skips carry fine detail across; the middle sees the whole digit at 7 × 7.

our model feature maps

The down path: each level halves the resolution and doubles the channels (block height ~ resolution, width ~ channels).

The middle: 128 channels at 7 × 7, where every unit sees most of the image.

The up path mirrors it back to 28 × 28, then a 1 × 1 convolution gives one number per pixel.

Skip connections concatenate the encoder's features into the decoder at the same resolution, so detail lost in the bottleneck is available again.

Real channels from the trained network on our noisy 7: edges early, blobs in the middle, a 7-shaped map at the end. This is a teaching-sized network; real models such as Stable Diffusion v1's U-Net have about 860 million parameters and attention layers at low resolution.

Telling the network which step it is at

t becomes a vector, then a per-channel offset in every block

sin and cos of t at 32 frequencies, like a Transformer's position encoding

Every block can change its behaviour with the noise level, at almost no cost in parameters.

The raw number t is turned into 64 features. Same idea as positional encoding: a single integer becomes a rich vector.

A small shared MLP, then in each block a linear layer maps it to that block's channel count.

The result is added to every pixel of the block's feature map. This is the line from our model's code.

Why do large models denoise a compressed image?

786,432 numbers → 16,384. Positions shrink by 8 × 8 = 64; the latent keeps 4 channels instead of 3, so numbers shrink by 48.

The latent is still spatial (a small image), so the same U-Net or DiT works on it. The autoencoder handles fine pixel detail once.

published SD v1 shapes

Stable Diffusion is a latent diffusion model: an autoencoder compresses each image, and diffusion runs in that latent space.

512² / 64² = 64.

But 3 channels become 4, so the total count falls by 48. Two ratios that students often mix up. These are representation sizes, not measured speedups.

Our own version: a small conv autoencoder for MNIST. Next slides show it working.

Latent diffusion, trained in two stages

First an autoencoder, then diffusion on its latents

The diffusion target is noise in latent coordinates; nothing about the training step changes.

our model

Stage 1: a convolutional autoencoder trained only to reconstruct (a VAE in Stable Diffusion; a plain autoencoder for us). Our 7's four latent channels are shown: a 7 × 7 picture each. The reconstruction is close to the input (MSE {{ae.mse}}).

Stage 2: freeze it. Encode every training image, divide by the latents' standard deviation so they have unit scale (Stable Diffusion uses a constant 0.18215 for the same reason), and train the same DDPM step on those latents.

Generation in latent space

Denoise latents for 1000 steps, decode once

No encoder at generation time: text-to-image starts from latent noise. Image-to-image tasks (SDEdit) can start from an encoded input instead.

our model

z_T is 4 × 7 × 7 Gaussian noise. The latent denoiser runs the usual loop, then the decoder runs once to produce pixels. Notation: z_t in latent space, x_t in pixel space.

Eight samples from our latent model. Quality is a little below the pixel model at this tiny scale; the gain is that each of the 1000 steps works on 196 numbers instead of 784. At 512 × 512 that gain is what makes the method affordable.

ViT returns as a denoiser

A Transformer can be the denoiser too: DiT

Patches in, a prediction for every patch out: no class token, because the output is dense. t and the label set each block's scale and shift.

published Peebles & Xie 2023 shapes

DiT: Peebles & Xie, Scalable Diffusion Models with Transformers, 2023 (class-conditional, latent space, 256 × 256 images).

DiT works on a latent of 4 × 32 × 32, exactly the kind of compressed image we just built. Cut into 2 × 2 patches: 256 tokens of 16 numbers each, exactly like ViT's patch embedding.

Full self-attention among all patches. Recall the earlier lecture: no causal mask, every patch sees every patch.

Conditioning: the step and the class label produce a scale and a shift for each block's normalisation (adaLN-Zero). This is a different conditioning route from the text cross-attention we will see in Part 6.

Unpatchify back to the latent's shape. The prediction is noise for every patch.
