Can text be generated by denoising?

Pixels are numbers, so Gaussian noise makes sense; words are not. Goal of this part: a different kind of corruption for tokens, with the rest of the recipe unchanged.

Why not add Gaussian noise to token IDs?

Token IDs are arbitrary labels; arithmetic on them means nothing

Add the same noise, +1.6, to “dog” and round: in A, 24 → 25.6 → 26, some unrelated word; in B, 2 → 3.6 → 4 = “ball”.

corrupt by maskingThe?chased?ball

Numbers near 24 are not words near “dog”. For discrete data, corrupt by hiding tokens instead.

The same Gaussian corruption turns "dog" into different words under different numberings. A noise process should not depend on an arbitrary labelling. (Diffusion on continuous word embeddings exists, e.g. Diffusion-LM; this section teaches the masked approach.)

Masking: each token is either kept or replaced by a special [MASK]. "How noisy" becomes "what fraction is hidden".

Same pattern as images: corrupt by a random amount, predict what was removed. The model sees the whole sequence in both directions.

illustrative probabilities

A clean sentence.

Draw a corruption level t between 0 and 1 (here 0.4) and hide each token independently with that probability. Here two tokens are hidden.

A bidirectional transformer outputs, at each position, a probability over the whole vocabulary: logits of shape [B, L, V]. Only the masked positions matter. These probabilities are illustrative.

Cross-entropy at the masked positions, summed, times 1/t: the LLaDA objective (Nie et al. 2025). The 1/t weight comes from the corresponding ELBO, like the w_t of Part 2. With t = 0.4, the sum 0.868 becomes 2.17.

illustrative a scripted trace

The prompt "The dog" is fixed; we choose a response length of 3 and mask all three slots. The length has to be chosen in advance (or a maximum, with an end token).

One forward pass predicts every hidden slot. Keep the most confident prediction ("ball", 0.71) and leave the others masked. This is low-confidence remasking (LLaDA): less confident predictions are thrown away and re-predicted.

With "ball" known, the remaining slots become confident; here both pass a threshold and are fixed together. Three tokens in two passes, in the order 5, then 3 and 4. The probabilities are a scripted illustration, not a trained model's output.

Fewer passes is not automatically faster: each masked-diffusion pass processes the whole sequence, and autoregressive models reuse cached work.

Be careful with speed claims: wall-clock time depends on sequence length, the number of steps, KV caching and hardware. What changes for sure is the dependency structure, not necessarily the latency.
