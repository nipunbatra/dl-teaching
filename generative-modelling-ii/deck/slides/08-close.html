The four questions, answered

The whole method on one slide

  1. Training input: $x_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon$ for a random t
    our pixel: 0.8·1 + 0.6·(−0.5) = 0.5
  2. Target: the noise $\epsilon$, with squared error
    best possible guess: an average, −0.24
  3. Sampling: from $x_T \sim \mathcal N(0, I)$, repeat mean + $\sigma_t z$ down to t = 1
    one step: 0.5 → 0.708 → 0.808
  4. Why: each ELBO term is a weighted noise MSE; DDPM drops the weights
    our pixel: $0.625 \times 0.1^2 = 0.00625$

Removing noise creates an image because each small step is a sample from the learned reverse distribution, and the steps add up.

Check yourself

Three questions to answer before next class

  1. Does training require running the reverse chain? No. One random t, one jump-formula input, one network call, one MSE per example.
  2. Why is the guess $\hat x_0$ different from the next state $x_{t-1}$? $\hat x_0$ is a full jump to a clean estimate, unreliable at high noise; $x_{t-1}$ moves only a little towards it, adds fresh noise, and is the state fed back in.
  3. What changes when the data are tokens? Corruption becomes masking and the loss becomes cross-entropy on masked positions; the sampler unmasks confident slots over several passes.
