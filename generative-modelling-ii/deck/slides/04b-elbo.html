Why is noise MSE the right loss?

Part 2 chose “predict the noise” because it is easy to set up. Now that Part 3 has given us both reverse means, the true one and the model's, we can show it is what the ELBO from Lecture I asks for, up to a weighting.

Lecture I, side by side with today

A VAE has one latent; diffusion has a chain of them

If diffusion is a VAE with a fixed encoder, the VAE's training objective, the ELBO, should apply to it.

Recall Lecture I: an encoder maps our digit to a short code z, a decoder maps it back, and z is pulled towards N(0, I).

Diffusion has the same shape, stretched: the noisy images are the latents, the noising chain plays the encoder and has nothing to learn, and the reverse network is a decoder applied T times. The end of the chain is N(0, I), the prior.

That suggests the question for this part: what does the ELBO say when the encoder is fixed?

Same ELBO derivation as Lecture I. The new part is how it splits up, one term per step.

Recall the VAE: encoder, decoder, prior, ELBO.

Diffusion's latents are the noisy images. The "encoder" is our fixed noising recipe, so there is no encoder network and no reparameterisation needed for it.

The decoder is the reverse step, and the same network is reused at every t. The prior is the end of the chain, N(0, I).

The bound is derived exactly as in Lecture I (appendix A3 writes it out). We now take it apart.

Taking the bound apart

The bound splits into one comparison per step

$-\text{ELBO} = \cz{L_T} + \sum_{t=2}^{T}\cdec{L_{t-1}} + \cx{L_0}$
$\cdec{L_{t-1}} = \mathbb E_{q}\, D_{KL}\big(\,q(x_{t-1}\mid x_t, x_0)\;\|\;p_\theta(x_{t-1}\mid x_t)\,\big)$

$L_T$ has no parameters; $L_0$ is a small pixel-level term. Training is really about the middle terms.

L_T: does the end of the forward chain match N(0, I)? With ᾱ_T = 0.00004 it almost exactly does, and there is nothing to learn.

The middle terms compare, at each step, the network's reverse step with the "true" reverse step q(x_{t−1} | x_t, x₀). That true step is a Gaussian we can write down because we know x₀ during training (appendix A2).

L₀ turns the last continuous state into pixel values. DDPM treats it separately. Next: one middle term, with numbers.

One middle term, for our toy pixel at t = 2

Equal variances: KL is the squared gap between the means

$D_{KL} = \dfrac{(\tilde\mu - \mu_\theta)^2}{2\sigma^2}$

true reverse mean $\tilde\mu = {{toy.mu_t}}$ (we know $x_0 = 1$)
model's mean $\mu_\theta = {{toy.mu}}$ (uses $\hat\epsilon = -0.4$)
both variances $\sigma^2 = {{toy.bt}}$

$D_{KL} = \dfrac{{{toy.gap4}}^2}{2 \times {{toy.bt}}} = {{toy.kl}}$

Training pushes the model's reverse mean onto the true one. Next: rewrite both means in terms of noise.

For Gaussians with equal variances, KL reduces to the squared difference of means over 2σ². The variance is fixed (not learned), so only the mean matters.

Both numbers come from Part 3: the true reverse step from x₂ = 0.5 (knowing x₀ = 1) has mean 0.745; the model, with ε̂ = −0.4, proposes 0.708.

0.037² / 0.222 = 0.00625. Keep this number; it reappears on the next slide.

The qualification

DDPM keeps the squared error and drops the weights

$L_{\text{simple}} = \mathbb E_{x_0, t, \epsilon}\,\|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)\|^2$

The bound's weight falls about {{weights.ratio}}× from t = 2 to t = 500. Setting every weight to 1 gives relatively more weight to noisy steps than the bound does.

It also drops $L_T$ (no parameters) and the discrete pixel term $L_0$.

The training loss is not the ELBO; it is a re-weighted sum of its per-step terms, chosen because it gave better samples (Ho et al. 2020).

computed

Red: w_t on the real schedule (t ≥ 2; the formula is undefined at t = 1 because the posterior variance is 0 there). Teal: the weight 1 the simple loss uses.

The bound cares most about the last few low-noise steps, which mostly polish imperceptible detail. The simple loss spends more effort on mid and high noise, where the content of the image is decided.

So, honestly: L_simple is motivated by the ELBO but is not equal to it. If you need likelihoods (bits per dimension), you must use the weighted bound or a learned variance (Nichol & Dhariwal 2021).

What we showed, and what comes next

What we did

  1. Diffusion is a VAE whose encoder is the fixed noising chain.
  2. Its ELBO is one KL per reverse step.
  3. Each KL is a weighted squared error on the noise: $0.625 \times 0.1^2$ for our pixel.
  4. DDPM trains with weight 1 at every step instead.
