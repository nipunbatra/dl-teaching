# Lecture 22 · Diffusion Models — Practice · Teaching Guide
*First-time-instructor companion. Audience: undergrads, one ML course.*

## The spine (say this in 2 sentences)
Three engineering tricks turn the toy DDPM of L21 into Stable Diffusion: **cross-attention** to a frozen text encoder (so a prompt steers the denoiser), **classifier-free guidance** (extrapolate along the prompt direction to make samples obey the text), and **latent diffusion** (run the whole loop in a 48×-smaller VAE latent instead of pixels). Then **DDIM** reuses the *same trained weights* with a deterministic sampler to cut 1000 steps down to ~25–50.

## Where it sits
Builds on … L21 DDPM (the ε-predictor and the reverse loop this lecture conditions and accelerates) · L13 attention (cross-attention is the same Q/K/V math, queries from image features, keys/values from text) · L18 CLIP (the frozen text encoder) · L19 VAE (the frozen encoder/decoder that makes the latent) · L20 (the VAE here is trained with perceptual + adversarial losses — a GAN cameo).
Sets up … L23 (efficient inference reuses the "doubled forward pass" cost idea; both lectures are about making models cheap to *run*). This is a "landscape/literacy" lecture — breadth over derivation; the only ⭐⭐⭐ math is the CFG formula.

## 80-minute plan
- ⭐ Core (~45 min): three ways to inject a condition (concat / add-to-time / cross-attention) and why cross-attention wins · cross-attention as "each image patch asks which prompt word matters to it" (the worked `red square` numeric) · classifier-free guidance — the two-compass intuition and the formula `ε_cfg = ε_∅ + w(ε_c − ε_∅)` · the CFG-ready 10%-dropout training trick · latent diffusion (the zip-the-file analogy + the 48× dimension math) · the three SD components (frozen VAE, frozen CLIP, trainable U-Net).
- ⭐⭐ Should-cover (~25 min): the CFG-scale table (w=1 → pure conditional, 7.5 → SD default, 25+ → fried) · why DDPM needs ~1000 steps · DDIM as "see the whole path, take 50 confident strides, no retraining" · DiT (patchify the latent, treat patches as tokens, condition via adaLN) and why Transformers scale where U-Nets plateau.
- ⭐⭐⭐ Optional / skip-if-tight: the CFG step-by-step derivation slide · the sampler menu (DPM-Solver++, flow matching, consistency models) · consistency-model "teleporter" intuition. One-line gist if asked about consistency models: "distill the whole 50-step trajectory into a network that jumps from any noise level straight to `x_0` in one shot — GAN speed at near-diffusion quality."
**Do live on the board:** the **CFG vector extrapolation**. Use 2D: `ε_∅ = [0.10, −0.30]`, `ε_c = [0.40, −0.10]`. Difference (pure prompt influence) `Δ = ε_c − ε_∅ = [0.30, 0.20]`. Then `ε_cfg = ε_∅ + wΔ`: w=1 → `[0.40, −0.10]` (exactly conditional), w=3 → `[1.00, 0.30]`, w=7 → `[2.20, 1.10]`. Draw the arrows: the magnitude *explodes* as w grows. This one picture explains both why high w follows the prompt fiercely **and** why very high w "deep-fries" the image (it extrapolates the noise prediction clean out of the natural-image distribution).

## Teach it like this
Open with "how does *an astronaut riding a horse* become an image?" and lock in the answer: a frozen CLIP encoder makes a prompt vector, and the U-Net cross-attends to it at every layer — change the vector, change the image, *with no retraining*. Build cross-attention as "a team of painters, each asking which word matters for their patch," then do the `red square` numeric. Hit the natural objection — "conditioning is in, so why do samples still half-ignore the prompt?" — and answer it with CFG and the board extrapolation. Then motivate latent diffusion as a cost problem (1000 passes on 786k pixels is insane; a VAE zips it 48×), and close with the speed story: DDIM (same weights, 20× fewer steps) and DiT (the backbone of Sora/SD3). Payoff: "Stable Diffusion, SD3, Flux, Sora — all the *same* L21 recipe with bigger backbones and these four tricks bolted on."

## Heads-up for YOU (subtle points to get right)  ← most important section
- **CFG is NOT a probability or a Bayes update — it's a geometric extrapolation in noise/score space.** `ε_∅ + w(ε_c − ε_∅)` with `w > 1` is just walking *past* the conditional prediction along the prompt direction. That's precisely why high `w` pushes samples *out of distribution* (oversaturation, "deep-fried" artifacts): you're amplifying a vector with no guarantee the result corresponds to any real image. Don't justify CFG as "sharpening the posterior" — it's an algebraic hack that happens to work brilliantly in the useful range.
- **CFG strictly DOUBLES inference compute.** Every denoising step runs the U-Net twice — once with the prompt, once with the null embedding. "50 steps with CFG" is 100 heavy forward passes. The L21 cost story changes the moment you turn CFG on; say so.
- **`w = 1` is the *pure conditional* prediction, and `w = 0` ignores the prompt.** Many people misread the scale. `w=0` → unconditional, `w=1` → exactly `ε_c` (no extrapolation), `w>1` → overshoot. The "guidance scale 7.5" everyone quotes is already 6.5 units of *extrapolation past* the conditional prediction.
- **The latent-diffusion VAE is nearly LOSSLESS, not magic lossy compression — and it's trained separately and frozen.** Two distinct misconceptions to kill: (1) it's not the blurry vanilla VAE of L19 — SD's VAE uses perceptual + adversarial (GAN) losses and only does an 8× *spatial* downsample (512→64), discarding imperceptible high-frequency detail while keeping structure essentially intact. (2) It is *pre-trained and frozen*; the diffusion U-Net trains entirely in the fixed latent space. Saying "the VAE is trained jointly with the diffusion model" is wrong.
- **DDIM is an inference-time SAMPLER, not a new model or architecture — that's why no retraining is needed.** The U-Net predicts the *total* ε (→ an estimate of `x_0`); DDIM uses that to analytically re-noise `x_0` to *any* chosen intermediate timestep, and since the network takes `t` as input it handles the new noise level fine. So "skipping steps" is just choosing a sparse subset of timesteps to evaluate the same weights. DDIM also makes sampling *deterministic* (it sets the reverse-step noise `σ_t = 0`), which is what enables reproducible latents and image-to-image.
- **Cross-attention is *cross*, not self — and it's where the prompt enters.** Self-attention inside the U-Net mixes spatial positions (image talking to itself). Cross-attention has queries from image features but keys/values from the *text* embedding — that's the only place conditioning information flows in. If you conflate them, the "how does text get in?" story collapses.
- **DiT conditions via adaLN (adaptive LayerNorm), not by concatenating tokens.** Time and class/text modulate the LayerNorm scale/shift parameters of each Transformer block — a small but specific detail. The reason DiT scales: it inherits the entire LLM systems stack (FlashAttention, tensor parallelism) and Transformers keep improving with compute where U-Nets plateau.

## Where students stumble (and the fix)
- **"Why run the model twice for CFG — isn't conditional enough?"** Conditional alone (`w=1`) often half-ignores the prompt. You need the *unconditional* prediction to compute the difference vector `ε_c − ε_∅`, which isolates the prompt's pure contribution; then you amplify *that*. Scaling `ε_c` alone just changes total noise magnitude, not the semantic direction — say this explicitly, it's the most common confusion.
- **Thinking latent diffusion loses quality because "compression."** The compression is ~lossless *perceptually* (8× spatial, GAN/perceptual-trained VAE). It throws away the redundant stuff (a sky's hundreds of near-identical blue pixels), not the structure. Use the zip analogy: zip → diffuse in the small space → unzip; the zip is reversible enough that you can't see the difference.
- **Believing DDIM is "lower quality because fewer steps."** 50 DDIM steps ≈ 1000 DDPM steps in quality — it's a smarter integrator of the same reverse process, not a coarser approximation. The quality drop only appears at very aggressive step counts.
- **Reading the cross-attention shapes wrong.** Queries are `[image_positions, d]` (one per spatial location), keys/values are `[77, d]` (one per CLIP token). The attention matrix is `[image_positions, 77]` — each pixel-region attending over the 77 prompt tokens. Draw the shapes.

## If a student asks…
- "If `w > 1` pushes the prediction *past* the conditional, why doesn't it always produce garbage?" → In the moderate range (≈3–10) it pushes into regions that more strongly match the prompt, sharpening adherence. Too far (w≈25+) and the extrapolation leaves the natural-image manifold → oversaturated, broken outputs. It's a hyperparameter you sweep; SD defaults to 7.5.
- "Why does latent diffusion not blur like the VAE we saw in L19?" → SD's VAE compresses only 8× spatially and is trained with perceptual + adversarial losses, so reconstructions are perceptually near-lossless. L19's blur came from a heavy bottleneck and pure-likelihood objective; this VAE is a high-fidelity codec, not an aggressive compressor.
- "(Hard) In DDIM, if I jump from t=999 straight to t=950, doesn't the model expect the exact next step?" → No — the U-Net predicts the *total* ε to get back to `x_0`, not the increment to `x_{t−1}`. DDIM estimates `x_0` from that ε, then uses the *exact* forward formula to re-noise `x_0` to whatever timestep you pick (950), and feeds that timestep in as input. The model never assumes adjacency, so you can skip freely.
- "Does CFG need a special training run?" → It needs one cheap modification: during training, drop the prompt to the null embedding ~10% of the time so the *same* network learns both the conditional and unconditional paths. No second model — you just run the one network twice at inference.
- "Why are Transformers (DiT) replacing U-Nets — wasn't conv's local bias good for images?" → Local bias helps at small scale/data, but at frontier scale Transformers scale better with compute and data, give every patch immediate global communication, and plug straight into the LLM optimization ecosystem (FlashAttention, parallelism). U-Nets plateau; DiT doesn't. Tie to the L13 "Transformers scale" master sentence.
- "Is text generated by diffusion too?" → Mostly no — autoregressive LLMs still win on text. Diffusion conquered every signal you can smoothly *noise* (images, video, audio, 3D, molecules, robot actions); discrete text is the big holdout (though discrete-diffusion text models are an active research area).

## If you're short on time
- **Cut:** the CFG step-by-step derivation slide (the board example covers it), the full sampler menu, consistency models and flow matching (mention "few-step samplers are closing the speed gap" in one sentence), the DiT internals (keep "Transformer backbone, scales better, powers Sora/SD3").
- **Never cut:** cross-attention as the conditioning mechanism, the CFG formula + board extrapolation, the latent-diffusion 48× dimension math, and the DDIM "same weights, fewer steps, no retraining" fact. Those four are the literal title of the lecture.

## Closing line
"Stable Diffusion isn't a new idea — it's L21's denoiser plus four pieces of engineering: cross-attend to a text vector, extrapolate along the prompt, **diffuse where the information lives (the latent, not the pixels)**, and skip 95% of the steps for free. Next lecture: the model's trained — now make it cheap enough to actually serve."
