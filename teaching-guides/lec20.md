# Lecture 20 · GANs · Teaching Guide
*First-time-instructor companion. Audience: undergrads, one ML course.*

## The spine (say this in 2 sentences)
A GAN is two networks playing a minimax game: a Generator that turns noise into fake images, and a Discriminator that tries to tell fake from real — and **the Discriminator's gradient is the only training signal the Generator ever gets**. The whole lecture is one question stated four ways: this game has no fixed loss landscape, so how do we make it converge (non-saturating loss → DCGAN cookbook → WGAN)?

## Where it sits
Builds on … L19 VAE (the other answer to "how do you train a generator with no labels"; contrast: VAE optimizes a likelihood/ELBO, GAN optimizes nothing you can write as a single loss) · binary cross-entropy from intro-ML (D is literally a BCE classifier) · CNNs/BatchNorm (L7–L9, the DCGAN body).
Sets up … L21 diffusion, which is pitched explicitly as "GANs without the adversary" — a *static* regression target instead of a moving discriminator. The closing line ("when you can't write the loss, train a network to be the loss") is the bridge to RLHF reward models (L16) and beyond.

## 80-minute plan
- ⭐ Core (~45 min): the counterfeiter/detective intuition · the minimax objective derived from BCE (D maximizes `log D(x) + log(1−D(G(z)))`, G minimizes it) · why `.detach()` matters in the D-update · the **non-saturating G loss** and the gradient-gap numeric · why GAN training is fundamentally hard (no fixed landscape) · mode collapse (mechanism + visual) · the DCGAN five-rule cookbook at a glance.
- ⭐⭐ Should-cover (~25 min): the optimal-D derivation `D* = p_data/(p_data+p_G)` and the "GAN ≈ minimize JS" result (needed to *motivate* WGAN) · Wasserstein / earth-mover intuition · WGAN critic-with-a-speed-limit (1-Lipschitz) · FID in one paragraph · the GAN-vs-diffusion trade-off table.
- ⭐⭐⭐ Optional / skip-if-tight: the full WGAN-GP gradient-penalty term-by-term · StyleGAN's w-space hierarchy and style mixing · the Kantorovich–Rubinstein duality line. One-line gist if asked about WGAN-GP: "we want the critic's slope to be exactly 1 everywhere; we can't check everywhere, so we spot-check random points between a real and a fake and penalize any slope ≠ 1."
**Do live on the board:** the **saturating vs non-saturating gradient gap**. Let D's pre-sigmoid output be `a`, so `D(G(z)) = σ(a)`, and take D confident on a fake: `σ(a) = 0.01`. Saturating loss `L = log(1−σ(a))` → `∂L/∂a = −σ(a) = −0.01`. Non-saturating loss `L = −log σ(a)` → `∂L/∂a = σ(a) − 1 = −0.99`. The non-saturating gradient is ~100× stronger *exactly when G is terrible and needs the signal most*. This single derivation justifies why everyone uses the footnote version — it lands because it's two lines of chain rule and the punchline is a 100× number.

## Teach it like this
Open with the counterfeiter/detective pop quiz and force the answer "they train each other" — that's the entire paradigm in one sentence. Then derive the objective *from BCE they already know* (D is just a classifier), so the scary `min max V(D,G)` is demystified as "the same loss, one player maximizing, the other minimizing." Do the non-saturating gradient on the board next, because it's the first place the "static-vs-moving target" theme bites. Spend the back half on instability: mode collapse (with the mechanism, not just the picture), then frame JS-divergence's flatness as *the* disease and WGAN as the cure. Payoff to say out loud: "GANs lost the crown to diffusion, but the durable idea — *a neural network can be a learned loss function* — outlived the model and reappears in RLHF, CycleGAN, and robust training."

## Heads-up for YOU (subtle points to get right)  ← most important section
- **The non-saturating GAN does NOT minimize JS divergence.** The clean "GAN ≈ minimize Jensen–Shannon" result holds only for the *saturating* objective `min log(1−D(G(z)))` — which nobody uses. The non-saturating loss everyone actually trains optimizes a different, asymmetric divergence (informally `KL − 2·JS`, the Arjovsky/"Towards Principled GANs" analysis). So state it precisely: "the original *theory* says JS; the loss we *use* doesn't, and that mismatch is part of why JS theory got abandoned for Wasserstein." Don't claim trained GANs minimize JS.
- **Mode collapse ≠ mode dropping.** Mode dropping = the model misses some modes (e.g. generates 8 of 10 MNIST digits). Mode collapse is the pathological extreme: G maps many/all noise vectors to (nearly) one output. The slides use "mode collapse" for both — when a student asks about the spectrum, give them the two words. The fix toolbox (minibatch discrimination, WGAN, etc.) targets both but they're different severities.
- **In WGAN, D is a CRITIC, not a classifier — it outputs an unbounded real score, not a probability.** No sigmoid, no log, no "0 = fake / 1 = real." Saying "the WGAN discriminator predicts 0.2 for the fake" is just wrong. It scores realness; the loss is `E[D(real)] − E[D(fake)]` (raw scores). This is the single most common imprecision when an instructor carries vanilla-GAN intuition into the WGAN slides.
- **The 1-Lipschitz constraint is load-bearing, not decorative.** Without it the critic would send real → +∞ and fake → −∞, the loss diverges, and you've recreated the exact infinitely-steep, zero-useful-gradient cliff WGAN was built to avoid. The cap on the slope is *what makes the gradient gentle and always non-zero* when supports don't overlap. If a student asks "why slope ≤ 1 specifically?" — the 1 is a scale convention (Kantorovich–Rubinstein); any constant Lipschitz bound gives the Wasserstein distance up to that scale.
- **Original WGAN used weight clipping, and it was a bad idea — GP replaced it.** The 2017 WGAN paper enforced Lipschitz by clamping weights to `[−c, c]`, which pushes weights to the clip boundaries and cripples capacity. WGAN-GP (gradient penalty) is the version to teach and the version people use. Don't present weight clipping as the recommended method.
- **WGAN fixed the *gradient* problem, not the *game* problem.** It does not guarantee convergence — it's still a two-player non-cooperative game with no SGD-style guarantee, and WGANs can still oscillate/diverge. The slides say this; make sure you don't oversell WGAN as "the fix for GAN training."
- **`.detach()` is about not updating G during the D-step, not about correctness of the loss value.** Without it, `loss_D.backward()` would also populate gradients on G's parameters; you'd be updating G with the *discriminator's* objective. It's a top-3 from-scratch GAN bug.

## Where students stumble (and the fix)
- **"Where does the Generator's loss come from if there are no labels for images?"** The label *is* the Discriminator's output. G never sees a target image; it only sees "how fake did D think you were, and which direction reduces that." Repeat the reframe: D is a *learned* loss function for G.
- **Thinking the equilibrium means D got "good."** At convergence D outputs ~0.5 everywhere — it has been reduced to a coin flip *because* G is perfect. A flatlining D near 0.5 is success, not failure. (Contrast: D loss → 0 means D is *winning too much* and G is starved — the bad case.)
- **Confusing "G fools D" (good) with "D loss is low" (often bad).** Walk the loss diagnostics explicitly: D loss hovering ~0.5/balanced is healthy; D loss crashing to 0 is the D-too-strong failure where G gets no gradient.
- **Reading the minimax as two separate losses to minimize.** It's *one* value function `V(D,G)`; D climbs it, G descends it. Same surface, opposite directions. Show it's literally the BCE objective with the sign of G's update flipped.

## If a student asks…
- "Does the Discriminator need to be better than the Generator, or vice versa?" → Slightly-ahead D is the healthy regime — it gives G a meaningful gradient. If D is too weak it outputs 0.5 and G learns nothing; if D is too strong (vanilla saturating loss) G's gradient vanishes. The non-saturating loss and WGAN both exist to keep G getting signal even when D is winning.
- "Why `max log D(G(z))` instead of `min log(1−D(G(z)))` — aren't they the same?" → Same global optimum, very different gradients. Near `D(G(z))≈0` (D confident, early training) the first is steep and the second is flat. Do the board numeric: ~0.01 vs ~0.99.
- "Can't D just penalize G for only making cats?" → Not in vanilla GANs — D scores one image at a time and a single fake cat *is* realistic, so it can't see the lack of diversity. Minibatch discrimination explicitly lets D look at a batch and spot "these 64 are all the same," which is why it fights mode collapse.
- "(Hard) Why must the WGAN critic be 1-Lipschitz — what breaks otherwise?" → Kantorovich–Rubinstein duality says the Wasserstein distance equals the max over **1-Lipschitz** critics of `E[D(real)] − E[D(fake)]`. Drop the constraint and the sup is `+∞` (push real up and fake down without limit), the loss diverges, and the gradient becomes the same useless cliff as JS. The Lipschitz cap is what guarantees a finite distance and a gentle, always-non-zero slope.
- "Is FID a loss we train on?" → No. FID is an *evaluation* metric: run real and fake images through a frozen Inception-V3, fit a Gaussian to each feature set, and take the Wasserstein-2 distance between those two Gaussians. It's measured, not optimized. Lower is better; ~2 is SOTA-diffusion territory.
- "If diffusion won, why study GANs?" → Two reasons. Practically, GANs are one forward pass (real-time) and have the cleanest latent-editing story (StyleGAN inversion), so they survive in niches. Conceptually, the adversarial idea — *use a critic network as the loss* — reappears everywhere (RLHF reward models, domain-adversarial training, actor-critic RL). The model retired; the idea didn't.

## If you're short on time
- **Cut:** WGAN-GP term-by-term, StyleGAN w-hierarchy/style-mixing, the Kantorovich–Rubinstein duality slide, the FID formula (keep the one-paragraph intuition).
- **Never cut:** the minimax-from-BCE derivation, the non-saturating gradient board example, mode collapse mechanism, and the WGAN *intuition* (critic with a speed limit) — even if you skip the math, students need "JS goes flat when supports don't overlap; Wasserstein doesn't" as the takeaway.

## Closing line
"GANs lost the sample-quality crown to diffusion in 2021 — but the one idea that outlived them is the deepest in the course: **when you can't write down the loss, train a network to be the loss.** Next lecture we delete the adversary entirely and replace it with a target you *can* write down — the noise you added."
