# Lecture 8 · Modern CNNs & Transfer Learning · Teaching Guide
*First-time-instructor companion. Audience: undergrads, one ML course.*

## The spine (say this in 2 sentences)
The Inception → ResNet → MobileNet → EfficientNet arc is one principle in four costumes — *find a way to add depth or width that pays for itself* — and the single most load-bearing idea in the whole arc is the **residual skip**, which lets a layer learn a small *correction* `F(x)` on top of an identity highway instead of relearning the whole mapping. The practical payoff is **transfer learning**: once a backbone has learned ImageNet's edges/textures/parts, you keep that as a learned prior, replace the head, and fine-tune — worth roughly one to two orders of magnitude of training data on most vision tasks.

## 80-minute plan
- ⭐ **Core (~45 min):** the 1000-image plant-disease pop-quiz (hold the answer) · **ResNet** — the degradation problem, the residual block $h_\text{out}=h_\text{in}+F(h_\text{in})$, the `+1` gradient highway with the worked numeric (plain → $5\times10^{-21}$ after 10 layers, residual → ~0.55) · why residual=0 (identity) is the *easy* thing to learn so adding blocks can only help · **transfer learning** — backbone/head/freeze vocabulary, the three recipes, the data-size → freeze-depth table. Every student must leave able to (a) explain *why* the skip fixes degradation and (b) pick freeze-vs-fine-tune from dataset size.
- ⭐⭐ **Should-cover (~25 min):** **1×1 bottleneck** (the 589k → 69.6k = ~8.5× param drop) · projection shortcuts (the adapter-plug analogy: 1×1 conv with matching stride when shapes disagree) · **depthwise separable** convs (split spatial from channel mixing, ~8× cheaper, MobileNet) · discriminative/layer-wise LRs (early layers slow, head fast) · the `timm` one-liner ecosystem.
- ⭐⭐⭐ **Optional / skip-if-tight:** the full 1×1 cost derivation · **EfficientNet compound scaling** ($d=\alpha^\phi,w=\beta^\phi,r=\gamma^\phi$, $\alpha\beta^2\gamma^2\approx2$) · the negative-transfer deep-dive · the pick-the-backbone table (DINOv2 etc.) · Squeeze-and-Excitation (P6). Gist if asked about EfficientNet: "instead of cranking *one* knob (depth or width or resolution), scale all three together in a fixed ratio so each doubling of compute is spent in balance — the *idea* outlived the architecture."
- **Do live on the board:** the **residual gradient numeric**. Upstream gradient 0.5, tiny weights so $F'(h)=0.01$. Plain after 10 layers: $0.5\cdot(0.01)^{10}\approx5\times10^{-21}$ (dead). Residual: $0.5\cdot(1.01)^{10}\approx0.55$ (alive). Then write the per-layer factor: plain is $F'$, residual is $1+F'$ — the `+1` is the highway, present *at construction time*, not earned through training luck. This is the emotional core of the lecture and the cleanest "why ResNet trains 152 layers" you can give.

## Teach it like this
Open with the pop-quiz (1000 labelled images, 5 plant-disease classes — train ResNet-50 from scratch? tiny CNN? pretrained + fine-tune? ViT?) and *don't* answer it. Pick up L07's cliffhanger: "you stacked more layers and *training* loss got worse — that's not overfitting, it's optimization." Build the residual block as the fix and *prove* it with the gradient numeric — the `+1` is the entire trick, and it reappears in LSTMs and Transformers. Then reframe the rest of the architectures as "the same move, paying for added capacity differently" (Inception widens in parallel with 1×1 bottlenecks; MobileNet factorizes spatial vs channel; EfficientNet scales three axes jointly). The payoff is transfer learning: a pretrained backbone is a *learned prior*, the three recipes are just "how much backbone do I freeze," and the answer is set by dataset size. Close on the revisited pop-quiz: (c) pretrained ResNet-50, replace head, fine-tune — the default for nearly any CV task under ~100k labels.

## Heads-up for YOU (subtle points to get right)   ← most important section
- **ResNet primarily fixes *degradation*, not "vanishing gradients" full stop — and there's nuance.** The motivating experiment (He 2015) is that *plain* deeper nets had higher **training** error than shallower ones — an optimization/degradation problem, since a deeper net can always represent a shallower one by setting extra layers to identity, yet plain nets couldn't *find* that solution. The skip makes identity the *default* (just push `F→0`), so optimization can find it. Vanishing gradients are part of the story (and BatchNorm already helped a lot with raw signal scale), but if you say "ResNet exists only to fix vanishing gradients," a sharp student will note BN largely handled that and the paper's headline result is *training-error degradation*. Safe framing: "skip connections make adding layers a no-op-by-default, so deeper ≥ shallower — and as a bonus the `+1` keeps gradients flowing."
- **The residual is "learn the correction, not the replacement."** $h_\text{out}=x+F(x)$ means the block only has to learn the *residual* $F(x)=h_\text{out}-x$. When the ideal mapping is close to identity, $F\approx0$ is trivial to learn — far easier than forcing a stack of conv+ReLU to *approximate identity* from scratch. That's the answer to "why is this easier?" (P5) and it's the whole intuition.
- **BatchNorm placement and frozen-backbone BN are two different traps.** (1) Order in a block is conv → BN → ReLU (BN before the non-linearity). (2) When you "freeze" a backbone with `requires_grad=False`, BN's *running statistics still update in train mode* — so a "frozen" backbone in `.train()` keeps drifting its BN buffers, and train-vs-eval accuracy diverges. Fix: call `.eval()` on the frozen backbone (not just freeze grads). This is the #1 silent transfer-learning bug.
- **Freezing = `requires_grad=False`; it is NOT the same as eval mode.** Freezing stops *gradient updates* to weights. Eval mode changes the *behavior* of BN/dropout. You usually need *both* on a frozen backbone. Saying "I froze it" without `.eval()` leaves BN running-stats live.
- **"Fine-tune everything" needs a *small* backbone LR, or you destroy the prior.** The pretrained weights are already good; hitting them with the head's large LR wrecks them in the first few steps (symptom: fine-tuned model does *worse* than a linear probe). Use discriminative LRs — head fast (e.g. 1e-3), backbone slow (e.g. 1e-5), and warm up. Don't present "unfreeze and train" without the LR caveat.
- **Transfer can be *negative* — it's not free.** Large domain gap (ImageNet photos → grayscale X-rays / satellite / microscopy) can make pretraining *hurt*: val loss starts higher than from-scratch, late layers never adapt. Always recommend a from-scratch baseline to compare. Don't teach "pretraining always helps."
- **The preprocessing contract is mandatory.** A pretrained model expects the *exact* resize + mean/std it was trained with. Feed 0–1 images to a model that expects ImageNet-normalized inputs and accuracy sits at chance with no error thrown. Use `Weights.transforms()` (torchvision) — it hands you the correct pipeline.
- **"Bottleneck" and "depthwise separable" are different cost tricks — don't merge them.** Bottleneck = 1×1 *squeeze channels* → 3×3 → 1×1 *expand* (reduce the depth the expensive 3×3 has to chew through). Depthwise separable = split *one* conv into depthwise (spatial, per-channel) + pointwise (1×1, channel-mixing). Both cut FLOPs; they attack different axes.

## Where students stumble (and the fix)
- **"ResNet fixes vanishing gradients" stated as the whole story.** Fix: lead with *degradation* (training error rose with plain depth), then add the gradient-highway bonus. Use the worked numeric for the gradient half and the "identity is the easy default" line for the degradation half.
- **Frozen backbone still in train mode → BN drift.** Symptom: train accuracy fine, eval collapses (or vice versa). Fix: `.eval()` on the frozen part; re-measure. Re-state that freeze ≠ eval.
- **Fine-tuning the whole net with one big LR.** Symptom: pretrained features wrecked, worse than linear probe. Fix: backbone LR ÷10 (or discriminative LRs), head LR unchanged; warm up.
- **Wrong normalization / forgot to replace the 1000-way head.** Symptom: pretrained model stuck at chance. Fix: `print(model.fc)` (is it still 1000-way?) and confirm transforms use the pretraining mean/std.
- **Froze *everything*, including the new head.** Symptom: loss never moves at all. Fix: count `requires_grad=True` params — the new head must be trainable.

## If a student asks…
- **"Does ResNet fix vanishing gradients or the degradation problem?"** (the hard one) Both, but the headline is degradation: plain deeper nets had higher *training* error, which can't be overfitting. The skip makes identity the default mapping (push `F→0`), so optimization finds the deeper-≥-shallower solution; the `+1` in the gradient is the bonus that keeps signal flowing. BatchNorm had already taken a big bite out of raw vanishing-gradient issues.
- **"Why is learning `F(x)≈0` easier than learning identity directly?"** Because `F≈0` means "do nothing," and zero is trivial to reach (weights → 0). Forcing a conv→BN→ReLU→conv stack to *output its own input* is a precise, hard target. Reframing the block around the identity makes the easy case actually easy.
- **"When do I freeze vs fine-tune?"** By data size. <100 labels: linear probe (freeze all but the head). 100–1k: fine-tune top layers. 1k–10k: fine-tune the whole backbone with discriminative LRs. >100k: from-scratch may even win. Rule: smaller data → freeze more.
- **"Why does fine-tuning sometimes do *worse* than just a linear probe?"** Backbone LR too high — you overwrote good pretrained features before the task signal could guide them. Drop the backbone LR ~10×, keep the head LR, add warmup.
- **"When does pretraining actually hurt (negative transfer)?"** Big domain gap: ImageNet → X-rays / satellite / microscopy, very different image sizes, or a tiny target set. Symptoms visible early: val loss above the from-scratch baseline, late layers not adapting. Fix: closer-domain pretraining (RadImageNet, SatCLIP) or self-supervised pretraining on your own unlabeled data.
- **"Bottleneck vs depthwise separable — what's the difference?"** Bottleneck squeezes *channels* with 1×1s so the 3×3 runs on fewer channels (ResNet-50). Depthwise separable splits *one* conv into spatial-only (depthwise) + channel-only (1×1 pointwise) (MobileNet). Different factorizations, both cheaper.
- **"What should I actually start from in 2026?"** `timm.create_model('resnet50', pretrained=True, num_classes=N)` is a safe default; for general-purpose features, DINOv2 (self-supervised on 142M images) frozen features often beat ImageNet-supervised backbones. Always honor the model's `transforms()`.

Cut: the EfficientNet compound-scaling math, the full 1×1 cost derivation, the negative-transfer deep-dive, the backbone-picker table, and Squeeze-and-Excitation. Never cut: the **residual block + the `+1` gradient numeric**, the **"learn the correction, not the replacement" / identity-as-default** intuition, the **three transfer recipes keyed to data size**, and the **frozen-BN + backbone-LR pitfalls** (those are the two bugs every student hits). Those carry both halves of the lecture and all of L09.

## Closing line
"Skip connections let layers learn corrections instead of replacements — and that one idea both makes 152-layer nets trainable and turns a pretrained backbone into a prior you can reuse for almost any vision task."
