Last lecture · the VAE

Maximise a lower bound on the likelihood:

$\log p_\theta(x) \;\ge\; \ELBO(x)$

Needs an encoder $q_\phi(z \mid x)$ to say which $z$ made each $x$.

our VAE, last lecture

Today · the GAN

No $p_\theta(x)$, no bound, no encoder.

A second network $D$ judges real vs fake, and $G$ is trained to fool it.

our GAN, today

Last lecture: how do we train $G$ when nobody tells us which image each $z$ should make?

The VAE's strokes smear, the GAN's are crisp. What changed is the training signal.

Recall in one breath: the VAE maximises the ELBO, a lower bound on log p(x), and needs an encoder during training. The GAN never writes down p(x) at all.

Honesty about the comparison: the VAE grid shows the decoder's pixel probabilities (as last lecture), the GAN grid shows G's grey-level output directly, and the two models differ in size and architecture. The blur-vs-sharp contrast is a well-known qualitative difference, not a controlled experiment.
