Training G through D

G never sees a real image and has no target output. Its only teacher is D: move each fake towards where D says "more real", and change G's weights so that it does.

Step 2 of each iteration: what would fool D?

G step: push every fake uphill on $D(x)$

$$\mathcal L_G = -\log D(\cdec{G(z)})$$

The same BCE, but now the fakes are labelled 1: "make $D$ call these real".

Our fake at $x = {{dloss.x_fake}}$: $D = {{dloss.D_fake}}$, so $\mathcal L_G = -\log {{dloss.D_fake}} = {{dloss.lg_fake}}$. Raising $D$ there lowers it.

Red arrows: which way $D(x)$ increases at each fake, from D's real slope at step {{dloss.step}}.

G cannot move the fakes directly. It changes its weights $\theta$ so that its outputs move along the arrows.

At step {{dloss.step}} D has learned that large x looks real (D rises towards bump B), so most fakes are pushed right. Arrows are scaled by the slope dD/dx of the recorded D at that step, computed on a grid; the fakes are a subsample of the 2,000 fixed-z samples.

Where G's gradient comes from

The chain rule runs through D

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal L_G}{\partial \theta} = \underbrace{\frac{\partial \mathcal L_G}{\partial D}}_{=\,-1/D} \cdot \underbrace{\frac{\partial D}{\partial x}}_{\text{slope of } D \text{ at the fake}} \cdot \underbrace{\frac{\partial x}{\partial \theta}}_{\text{how } \theta \text{ moves the fake}}, \qquad x = G_\theta(z)$$
$-1/D$ is always negative: raising $D$ always lowers $\mathcal L_G$. Our fake: $-1/{{dloss.D_fake}} = {{dloss.inv_D}}$.
$\partial D/\partial x$: which way is "more real" at this fake. For ours it is ${{dloss.slope}}$: more real to the right. Uses D's weights, does not change them.
$\partial x/\partial\theta$: ordinary backprop through $G$, spreading the push over G's weights.

First two factors for our fake: ${{dloss.inv_D}} \times {{dloss.slope}} = {{dloss.dLdx}}$. Negative, so a gradient step moves it right, towards bump B.

This is why the G step must not detach the fake: the middle factor needs the path through D. It is also why D's weights can stay frozen: we need the slope of D with respect to its input, not an update to D.

For one sample. Over a batch, G's gradient is the average of these per-fake terms. The slope is read from the recorded D at step {{dloss.step}} (finite differences on a grid), rounded as shown.

Update G, hold D fixed

G step: lock D's weights, keep the wire

D.requires_grad_(False)                 # lock D's weights, keep the path
loss_G = bce(D(G(torch.randn(B, zdim))), ones)   # fresh fakes, no detach
opt_G.zero_grad(); loss_G.backward(); opt_G.step(); D.requires_grad_(True)

Gradient must flow through D to reach G, so no detach and no torch.no_grad() here. requires_grad_(False) only stops D's own weights from collecting gradients; D is still differentiated with respect to its input. Strictly, opt_G.step() only touches G's weights, and the next D step starts with opt_D.zero_grad(), so in this exact code the lock mainly saves computing gradients for D's weights. It matters in code that accumulates gradients across steps, and it states the intent.

The most common GAN bug

Freezing is not detaching

Q. The wire from G to D is cut. Does G get any gradient from this loss?

Q. Now D's weights are locked but the wire is intact. Does G learn?

Detach the fake in the G step and loss_G still prints a number, but G never learns. A silent failure.

Quiz: which single change to the G-step code makes the generator receive no gradient? Answer: D(G(z).detach()), or wrapping the G step in torch.no_grad(). Both still print a loss.
